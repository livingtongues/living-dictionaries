# SEO / GEO review — 2026-08-01 (livingdictionaries.app)

*Second run of the monthly SEO/GEO discoverability loop. Read-only against production — no site code
changed. This run built the re-runnable check the first digest asked for, and for the first time
pulled **real Google Search Console data** (the property has data from 2026-07-08 onward), which
turns the loop's "target query benchmark" from guesswork into measurement.*

## TL;DR

- **Technical SEO is healthy.** 16 production URLs across 6 page-types crawled: titles,
  page-specific descriptions, single self-referencing canonicals, parsing JSON-LD with the right
  `@type`, live `og:image`, sitemap membership, and 200s for GPTBot / PerplexityBot / ClaudeBot /
  Googlebot / Bingbot. **Two real failures**, both on `/create-dictionary` (below). No regressions
  vs 2026-07-16.
- **The real story is in Search Console, and it is stark.** Living Dictionaries took **598,781
  impressions and 2,193 clicks** in the 28 days to 2026-07-29 — a **0.366% site CTR**. Impressions
  grew **8× in three weeks** (5.7k/day → 47k/day) while clicks stayed flat (~70/day → ~120/day).
  Every bit of that growth is entry pages ranking for word-lookup queries that nobody clicks:
  queries containing *"in english"* pulled **136,370 impressions and 28 clicks (0.02% CTR)**, while
  queries containing *"dictionary"* pulled 3,212 impressions and **242 clicks (7.53% CTR)**.
- **The single highest-leverage move is already running** — Jacob's 50/50 entry-`<title>` gloss test
  deployed 2026-07-31. I verified its integrity in production (48 sampled entries, arm assignment
  matches the committed hash exactly, zero leakage). **Protect it and read it out ~2026-08-21;
  do not ship competing entry-page title/description changes before then.**
- **The GEO gap has a name now:** LD has 187,362 pages that contain the answer to "how do you say
  water in Tutelo", and for that class of query the page that surfaces is never an entry page. On
  Google, "how do you say" / "how to say" / "word for" queries sit at **position 18–27 with zero
  clicks**. On Brave, the answer-intent queries return the *dictionary landing page* or nothing.

### The smoking gun: CTR by query family

Same 28-day window, whole property, split by what the searcher typed:

| query contains | impressions | clicks | CTR | avg position |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `dictionary` | 3,212 | 242 | **7.53%** | 6.6 |
| `language` | 5,661 | 102 | 1.80% | 7.9 |
| `translate` | 1,024 | 5 | 0.49% | 8.7 |
| `meaning` | 68,470 | 22 | **0.03%** | 10.2 |
| `in english` | 136,370 | 28 | **0.02%** | 9.6 |
| `word for` | 80 | 0 | 0% | 18.4 |
| `how to say` | 34 | 0 | 0% | 21.2 |
| `how do you say` | 19 | 0 | 0% | 26.7 |

Two things fall out of that table:

1. **Where LD is understood as a dictionary product, it converts well** (7.53% CTR). Where it is
   just a string match on a foreign word, it converts at 1/300th of that rate.
2. **Position is not the problem — the snippet is.** Entry pages average 1.003% CTR *even at
   positions 1–3*, where a normal result earns 25–30%:

| entry-page position band | queries | impressions | clicks | CTR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | 1,004 | 2,294 | 23 | 1.003% |
| 3–5 | 1,061 | 6,935 | 33 | 0.476% |
| 5–10 | 8,812 | 116,610 | 117 | 0.100% |
| 10–20 | 7,348 | 47,159 | 22 | 0.047% |
| 20+ | 6,775 | 11,009 | 6 | 0.055% |

A result at position 2 that earns 1% is a result whose title and description are not telling the
searcher it has their answer. That is precisely the hypothesis the running A/B test measures.

## Index coverage sample (new metric)

The sitemap index advertises **187,362 URLs across 222 child sitemaps** (last downloaded by Google
2026-07-26, 0 warnings, 0 errors). Search Console's URL Inspection API on a spread of 48 sampled URLs
(3 non-entry pages + 45 entry pages across 10 dictionaries):

- **Site + dictionary pages: 3/3 "Submitted and indexed"** (`/`, `/create-dictionary`,
  `/tutelo-saponi`), all with a correct Google-chosen canonical.
- **Entry pages outside tutelo-saponi: 27/32 "Submitted and indexed"** (84%) — plus 3 "Discovered –
  currently not indexed", 1 "Crawled – currently not indexed", 1 "unknown". Coverage is good and is
  *not* ordered by position in the sitemap index — dictionaries at the head (`ache`), middle
  (`kumyk`) and tail (`zapoteco-serrano`) of the index are all indexed.
- **tutelo-saponi entries: 0/13 sampled have ever been crawled** ("URL is unknown to Google"), even
  though its 1,685 entry URLs sit in a well-formed child sitemap with valid `lastmod` values and
  the dictionary itself is one of the site's best performers (881 impressions, 104 clicks, 4.5
  position). Only 7 of its entry URLs earn any impressions at all. Its sitemap XML validates, has
  no duplicate or malformed `<loc>`, serves `application/xml`, and the indexed entries *are* listed
  in it — so this is depth-of-crawl, not a defect I can name yet. **Flagged for a targeted probe
  next run**, not treated as broken.

## Experiment integrity: the entry-title A/B test

Jacob's 50/50 entry-`<title>` test (`site/src/routes/[dictionaryId]/entry/[entryId]/seo_entry_title.ts`,
deployed 2026-07-31, readout ~2026-08-21) is the most valuable thing on this board, so I verified it
is actually running correctly in production before recommending anything near it.

I sampled **48 live entry pages across 6 dictionaries**, re-derived each entry's arm from the
committed djb2 hash of its id, and compared it against the title production actually served:

- 23 entries assigned to the `gloss_title` arm → **23 served a gloss title** (`mąnįkte means
  “river bank” in English | Tutelo-Saponi Monacan Living Dictionary`).
- 25 entries assigned to `control` → **25 served the headword-only title**. Zero treatment leakage.
- Zero unexpected fallbacks (no gloss / over-long gloss) in the sample.

<Callout type="warn" title="Do not disturb until ~2026-08-21">
Any change to entry-page titles, meta descriptions, or JSON-LD before the readout contaminates a
three-week experiment that is measuring the single biggest lever on the site (a 0.141% → 0.5% entry
CTR would add ~1,700 clicks per 28 days — roughly 1.8× the site's current total click volume). The
correct action this month is *nothing* on entry pages.
</Callout>

## Ranked gaps (by expected impact)

1. **🟡 `/create-dictionary` is the site's best-converting page and is nearly invisible to crawlers
   and answer engines.** It earns 2,205 impressions / 150 clicks at **6.80% CTR** — the highest CTR
   of any page type — ranking 2.2 for "dictionary maker", 5.8 for "make your own dictionary", 2.1
   for "dictionary creator". Yet its server-rendered HTML contains **58 characters** of its own
   text ("Name of Language* This will be the name of the dictionary."), has **no `<h1>`**, **no
   JSON-LD**, and the URL is **in no sitemap** (site.xml lists only `/`, `/about`, `/dictionaries`,
   `/tutorials`). Google has indexed it anyway, so this is not an indexation emergency — but an
   answer engine asked "what's a good free tool to build a dictionary for my language?" has
   literally nothing to read on the page that ranks for that intent. Adding real server-rendered
   copy (what it is, who it's for, what you get, the steps), a `SoftwareApplication` or `FAQPage`
   JSON-LD block, and the URL to `site.xml` is small, safe, and touches nothing the A/B test measures.
   *(Sources: `site/src/routes/create-dictionary/`, `site/src/routes/sitemaps/[dict_id].xml/+server.ts`
   — the `site` branch hard-codes its four paths.)*
2. **🟡 Answer-shaped queries reach no entry page — the core GEO gap.** "how do you say", "how to
   say" and "word for" queries put LD at position 18–27 with 0 clicks on Google, and on Brave they
   return a dictionary landing page or nothing. LD's 187k entry pages are discoverable by
   *headword* string only; nothing on the site targets the gloss-side phrasing an English speaker
   actually types. The running title test is step one (it puts the gloss and the language name into
   the SERP line). Step two — **after** the readout — is the same treatment for the entry `<meta
   description>`, which today reads `n., English: family` and would serve both engines far better as
   a sentence. Do not start this before 2026-08-21.
3. **🟡 Generic homepage `<title>` (carried from 2026-07-16, still open).** The most-linked page on
   the site spends its title tag on the bare brand `Living Dictionaries` while ranking 6.3 with a
   3.53% CTR. A subject-bearing title would capture head-term intent without hurting brand recall.
   Cheap, isolated from the A/B test, and measurable in Search Console within weeks.
   *(Source: `site/src/lib/components/seo-title.ts` — the homepage deliberately returns `SITE_NAME`
   alone to avoid `Living Dictionaries | Living Dictionaries`; the fix is to give the home route a
   real title rather than to change that helper.)*
4. **⬜ Site-quality risk worth watching, no action yet.** A 187k-page corpus averaging 0.14% CTR at
   position 9 is the kind of pattern search engines eventually respond to by pulling back crawl and
   index depth — which would cost the dictionaries that *do* convert. The A/B readout is the right
   next data point; if the gloss title doesn't move entry CTR meaningfully, the honest follow-up
   question is whether every entry deserves an indexable page, or whether thin gloss-only entries
   should consolidate to the dictionary's entry-list page. **Flag only — do not act on this in
   2026-08.**
5. **⬜ tutelo-saponi's 1,685 entry URLs appear uncrawled.** 0/13 sampled are known to Google versus
   87% indexed elsewhere. No defect found in its sitemap, canonicals, robots or SSR. Next run:
   inspect 10 more of its entries plus 10 from two other dictionaries of similar size and age to
   confirm whether it is genuinely an outlier or an artifact of a small sample.

## Tooling — the re-runnable check now exists (uncommitted)

- <File path="site/scripts/seo-audit/audit.mjs" /> — crawls the priority page-types, asserts every

```bash
cd ~/code/living-dictionaries/site
```
